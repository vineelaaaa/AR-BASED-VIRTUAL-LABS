Rate this experiment:

A simple pendulum exhibits Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) for small angles. Its period — the time for one complete oscillation — depends only on the string length and local gravity, not on the mass of the bob or (for small angles) the amplitude.

T = 2π √(L / g)

Increasing the length L increases the period; increasing gravity g decreases it — which is why a pendulum swings slower on the Moon. The damping control simulates air resistance gradually reducing the swing amplitude.

Aim: To study Simple Harmonic Motion and verify how the period of a simple pendulum depends on its length and on gravitational acceleration.

Virtual apparatus: Rigid support stand, inextensible string, spherical bob, protractor for angle measurement — all modeled as manipulable 3D objects.
